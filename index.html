Delayed All the Time.
On Time, Occasionally.

Prague public transport, played as a disco track. Every transport mode is an instrument, traffic density sets the volume and delays detune the chords. The chorus isn't triggered by the clock but by the data: whenever the surface-transport delay climbs past 68 % of that day's own maximum, the city stops keeping up and the track lifts into the chorus. Five days, five moods. Pick one and hear the difference. Every musical decision comes from the data; only the hiss of the hats and the reverb tail are random.

Prague public transport, played as a techno track. The worse the network runs against a normal day, the fuller the arrangement: calm days are kick, bass and pad, extreme ones break down and come back with everything. Lateness sets the groove: straight, swung or half-time by how many stops are over 90 s late. The harmony climbs from home to the dominant, gets brighter and dirtier on the way up, and resolves when the city recovers. Nothing is random.

    Mixer · live at the playhead

      What you're hearing · v2
      • Density: the network's delay against a normal day of the same type (z). Below normal: kick, bass and pad only. Normal: the full groove. Over +1 SD: arp, shaker, a second lead voice, open hats. Over +2 SD: a two-bar breakdown, then everything.
      • Groove: the share of bus, tram and trolleybus stops more than 90 s late. Under 10 %: straight 16ths. 10–25 %: swung. Over 25 %: half-time with triplet fills.
      • Harmony and timbre: z climbs tonic → subdominant → dominant → tensions → secondary dominants, one chord per bar, voice-led. Each rung opens the filter and adds drive. Falling back home is a cadence.
      • Metro: deep kick and a rolling analogue bass. No metro, no beat.
      • Trams: dark hats and plucks; the plucks echo with feedback set by how much delay a tram carries from stop to stop at that hour.
      • Buses: dub chord stabs and the pad. Trolleybuses: claps and cowbell.
      • Voice of the City: one scale step per 30 s of city-wide delay, stepwise, never more than a fifth. Trains stay out of the band; the ferry rings A, the fifth of the key.

      What you're hearing
      • Metro: kick drum and bassline. Average delay 7 s, the most reliable pulse in the city. No metro, no beat.
      • Trams: hi-hats and funky sixteenth-note plucks.
      • Buses: chord stabs on the off-beat, the backbone of the sound. The later they run, the more detuned the chord.
      • Voice of the City: the lead. Its melody is the city-wide average delay quantised to the scale: the later Prague runs, the higher it sings. Always in tune.
      • Trains: kicked out of the band. Averaging 111 s late on a typical weekday, they played so out of tune they broke the whole song. Still shown in the grid; never getting the mic back.
      • Trolleybuses: claps on 2 & 4, cowbell in the rush.
      • Ferry: a bell. On a typical Tuesday it rings three times all day; in summer the river gets a whole verse of its own.

      Song structure

        Each day keeps v1's key rule: D, with the mode chosen by the day's average delay, from Lydian for calm days down to Phrygian for the Meltdown. Every state is decided once per bar from the data at the downbeat, with hysteresis, so nothing flickers; the thresholds are fixed in standard deviations and percentages, not tuned to the five days. The grid's bottom row shows the groove (colour) and density (height). Every number: docs/ENGINE_V2.md.

        Each day sings in its own key, chosen by its average delay: calm days float in Lydian, ordinary days groove in Dorian, and the worse the city runs, the darker the mode, all the way down to Phrygian for the Meltdown. The groove swings, and everything melodic pumps around the metro's kick drum. In the chorus a pad, an arpeggio and a tambourine join in, and the chords lift and change twice as fast. Right before it the beat cuts out for a two-beat break: snare roll, riser, crash, drop. The day plays on a loop: at midnight it starts over, because public transport does too.

        Data: Prague Integrated Transport (PID) stop-time history, provided by Golemio, the open-data platform of Operator ICT / City of Prague. Source records cover 15 March to 8 September 2025; the five days played here are aggregated into 5-minute windows per mode (event count, mean and 90th-percentile arrival delay). No raw records are redistributed. Please credit Golemio when reusing this material. A sonification companion to a master's thesis on delay cascades in Prague public transport.
